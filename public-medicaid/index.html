STATE ANNOUNCED HOLD-HARMLESS USE

Assessment begins; certain coverage losses are paused

For states that elect and receive approval for the option, the state still assesses whether applicable people meet the requirement or qualify for an exclusion or exception. During the approved period, the state may suspend denials and disenrollments based solely on failure to demonstrate community engagement.

CMS option: through June 30, 2027, or a shorter state-selected period.

NO APPROVED HOLD-HARMLESS PERIOD VERIFIED

January 1 remains the federal baseline

Unless a state has an approved hold-harmless period or another approved implementation option, the federal January 1, 2027 start date remains the baseline for applicable individuals. Check the state Medicaid agency for current instructions and notices.

A missing announcement in this guide does not prove that a state has declined the option.

A practical starting point

Five steps you can take now

You do not need to guess whether a federal change applies to you. Start with official notices and your state Medicaid agency.

  1. Keep your contact information current.Check your mailing address, phone number, and email with the state agency.
  2. Read every notice.Do not ignore renewal packets, requests for information, or messages from Medicaid.
  3. Keep records of qualifying activities.If you may be subject to the requirement, keep work, education, training, or volunteer records that the state says it accepts.
  4. Ask whether an exclusion or exception applies.Do not assume a general list answers your individual situation. Ask the state or a qualified assister.
  5. Use official assistance.Use the state agency's own application, renewal, and contact channels.

Quick answers

What people should know

Does this apply to everyone on Medicaid?

No. The federal community-engagement requirement applies to defined groups of adults, generally ages 19–64, in the Medicaid adult group and certain section 1115 demonstration populations, subject to exclusions and exceptions. Other Medicaid groups are not automatically subject to this requirement. Confirm with your state.

Does a six-month hold-harmless period mean I do not need to do anything?

No. A state using the option still assesses whether applicable people meet the requirement or qualify for an exclusion or exception. You must still complete other Medicaid renewals and respond to state notices. Follow the instructions from your state.

Can this website tell me whether I qualify for an exemption?

No. This guide does not make eligibility decisions or determine whether a particular exemption or exclusion applies. It helps you identify questions to ask and find official state information.

What if my state has not announced a decision?

That means the status has not been verified in this guide. It does not mean the state has declined the option. Check your state's Medicaid agency and return for updates.

Need an official answer?

Go directly to your state Medicaid agency.

Applications, eligibility decisions, renewals, notices, and deadlines are handled by state agencies—not by this guide.
