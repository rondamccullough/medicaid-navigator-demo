Medicaid 2027: practical guides and next steps

Medicaid is administered by states, and federal changes do not affect every member in the same way. These guides explain the general framework and direct readers to official sources for state-specific instructions.

Important: This library is general educational information. It cannot tell you whether a federal requirement applies to you or whether you qualify for an exclusion or exception. Your state Medicaid agency's notice and instructions control.

1. What is changing in 2027?

Federal law establishes community-engagement requirements for certain adults covered through the Medicaid adult group and certain section 1115 demonstration populations. The federal start date is January 1, 2027, unless a state has an approved option or a state selected an earlier implementation date. The requirement does not apply to every person enrolled in Medicaid.

CMS's June 2026 interim final rule describes qualifying activities and the groups covered by the law. The framework generally includes work, community service, participation in a qualifying work program, education, combinations of activities, or meeting a specified monthly income threshold. The federal framework also contains exclusions and exceptions. State systems and instructions determine how people are asked to report or verify information.

2. What does the optional six-month hold-harmless period mean?

On November 13, 2026, CMS issued State Medicaid Director Letter #26-006 offering states an optional hold-harmless period through June 30, 2027. A state that elects the option and obtains CMS approval may suspend eligibility denials and disenrollments based on failure to demonstrate community engagement during its approved period.

This is not a blanket suspension of the requirement. The state must still assess whether applicable individuals meet the requirement, are deemed compliant, or qualify for an exclusion. The option applies only to community-engagement-based denials and disenrollments; it does not pause other Medicaid eligibility requirements such as income or age. A state may choose a shorter period than June 30.

That is why the state selector on our homepage distinguishes a state announcement from verified CMS approval. An announcement that a state intends to participate is not proof that CMS has approved the state's mitigation plan.

3. How to prepare for a Medicaid renewal

A hold-harmless period does not remove the obligation to complete other renewal steps. If your state sends a renewal packet or request for information, follow the deadline and instructions in that notice. Do not assume the hold-harmless option protects someone from coverage loss for unrelated eligibility reasons.

  1. Confirm that your state Medicaid agency has your current mailing address, phone number, and email.
  2. Open and read all mail, electronic notices, and messages from the agency.
  3. Record renewal dates and response deadlines on a calendar.
  4. Use only the official state portal, phone number, or address listed by the agency.
  5. Keep copies or confirmations of information you submit.
  6. If you do not understand a notice, contact the agency or a qualified enrollment assister promptly.

4. What to do when you receive a Medicaid notice

Start by identifying what the notice is asking you to do. A notice may concern renewal, income, household information, residency, a request for verification, or community engagement. These are not interchangeable. A hold-harmless period relates only to certain denials or disenrollments based on community-engagement noncompliance during the approved period.

  1. Read the entire notice, including the date and response deadline.
  2. Identify the exact issue and any documents or information requested.
  3. Use the contact information printed on the notice or published by the state agency.
  4. Ask whether the request relates to community engagement or another eligibility factor.
  5. Keep a copy of the notice and a record of your response.

If you disagree with an eligibility decision, use the appeal or fair-hearing instructions in the official notice. This guide does not provide legal advice or file appeals.

5. Where to get official Medicaid help

Each state Medicaid agency is responsible for applications, eligibility decisions, renewals, replacement cards, and program questions. Use the federal Medicaid agency directory to locate the correct state contact. If your state uses a separate enrollment marketplace, the Medicaid agency can direct you to the correct channel.
