Medicaid 2027: practical guides and next steps

Medicaid is administered by states. Federal changes do not affect every member in the same way, and state instructions can differ. These guides explain the general federal framework and direct readers to official sources for current state-specific information.

Important: This is general educational information, not an eligibility decision or individualized benefits advice. The official notice and instructions from your state Medicaid agency control.

1. What is changing in 2027?

Section 71119 of Public Law 119-21 establishes community-engagement requirements for defined Medicaid “applicable individuals.” The federal implementation date is January 1, 2027, unless a state implements the requirement earlier under an approved option. The requirement does not apply to every person enrolled in Medicaid.

CMS's public guidance describes a pathway involving at least 80 hours per month of qualifying community-engagement activities. The listed activities include work, community service, qualifying work programs, and education, subject to federal definitions and state implementation. CMS also describes a monthly earnings pathway of at least $580 and a half-time education pathway. People may be excluded or qualify for exceptions. The precise rules, evidence, verification process, and applicability must be confirmed with the state.

Do not assume that every hour of work, school, training, or volunteering automatically counts. Ask the state which activities it accepts, what records it requires, and when it will verify information.

2. Who may be affected?

The federal law applies to defined groups of adults, including certain individuals covered through the Medicaid adult group and certain section 1115 demonstration populations. Other Medicaid coverage groups are not automatically subject to the requirement. The law and CMS guidance describe exclusions and exceptions, and states must have processes to identify people who qualify.

This website cannot determine your coverage group or whether an exclusion or exception applies. Ask your state Medicaid agency:

  • What Medicaid coverage group am I enrolled in or applying under?
  • Is that coverage group subject to community-engagement requirements in this state?
  • Does an exclusion or exception apply to my circumstances?
  • What activities count, what records are accepted, and when must I report them?
  • How will the state notify me and how can I correct an error?

3. What does the optional six-month hold-harmless period mean?

On November 13, 2026, CMS issued State Medicaid Director Letter #26-006 offering states an optional hold-harmless period through June 30, 2027. A state must elect the option and obtain CMS approval of its mitigation plan. An announcement of intent is not the same as approval.

During an approved period, a state may suspend eligibility denials and disenrollments based solely on failure to demonstrate community engagement. This is not a blanket pause on the requirement or on Medicaid eligibility rules. The state continues to assess applicable individuals, and other eligibility requirements, renewals, and response deadlines continue to matter.

States may choose a shorter period. The state tracker on the homepage distinguishes a public announcement from independently verified CMS approval. Where a status is not verified in our tracker, that does not mean the state declined.

4. How to use the monthly activity planner

The homepage planner lets you enter activity-hour totals in your own browser. It adds the amounts and compares them with an 80-hour planning reference. You can also enter monthly earnings to see the alternative pathway currently described in CMS's public guide.

The planner does not save or transmit your entries. It does not decide whether an activity qualifies, whether the requirement applies, or whether you have met a state verification standard. Use it to organize questions and records—not as proof of compliance.

5. How to prepare for a Medicaid renewal

A hold-harmless period does not remove the obligation to complete other renewal steps. If your state sends a renewal packet or request for information, follow the deadline and instructions in that notice.

  1. Confirm that your state Medicaid agency has your current mailing address, phone number, and email.
  2. Open and read all mail, electronic notices, and messages from the agency.
  3. Record renewal dates and response deadlines on a calendar.
  4. Use the official state portal, phone number, or address listed by the agency.
  5. Keep copies or confirmations of information you submit.
  6. If you do not understand a notice, contact the agency or a qualified enrollment assister promptly.

6. What to do when you receive a Medicaid notice

A notice may concern renewal, income, household information, residency, a request for verification, or community engagement. These are different issues. A hold-harmless period relates only to certain community-engagement-based denials and disenrollments during an approved period.

  1. Read the entire notice, including the date and response deadline.
  2. Identify the exact action requested and the reason for the notice.
  3. Use the contact information printed on the notice or published by the state agency.
  4. Ask whether the request concerns community engagement or another eligibility factor.
  5. Keep a copy of the notice and a record of your response.
  6. If you disagree with a decision, follow the appeal or fair-hearing instructions in the official notice.

7. Where to get official Medicaid help

Each state Medicaid agency handles applications, eligibility decisions, renewals, replacement cards, and program questions. Use the federal directory to locate your state agency. CMS also publishes state-specific Medicaid eligibility-change information.
